The problem

Every existing check verifies the number. None verify the voice.

Caller ID

Spoofable, and routinely spoofed. STIR/SHAKEN attests the originating line — not the human speaking on it.

"It sounds like him"

Modern neural TTS clones timbre from seconds of scraped audio. Under time pressure, human ears are not a control.

Call-back policy

Works — and is skipped exactly when it matters, because the attack is engineered to feel too urgent for it.

The asymmetry that makes this worse every month

The attacker needs a webinar recording and a free TTS account. The defender loses the full transfer. Any control that raises the attacker's cost above "trivial" changes the economics — and right now there is no such control on the voice itself.

Who is hit

  • Bank / NBFC desks approving telephonic transfers
  • Finance and HR staff taking "CEO" calls
  • Senior citizens receiving a familiar voice in distress

What the PS demands

  • Detection during live calls, not after
  • An actionable risk score before the transfer
  • Privacy-preserving and multilingual for India

Architecture

Four layers, and a rule about what silence means.

The rule that makes it work in the field

A layer that cannot run — no enrolment, no voiced speech, no metadata — reports confidence = 0 and is removed from both sides of the fusion. It never votes "genuine" by default. The standard ensemble treats a missing sub-model as 0.5, which drags every verdict toward the prior — and the layers most likely to be unavailable are exactly the ones whose absence coincides with an attack. That failure is silent, and it is in the attacker's favour.

The slide most decks leave out

Our accuracy numbers are too good, and here is why.

The shipped weights were trained on audio this repository generated

An EER of 0 % on a real corpus would be a red flag, not an achievement. Published state of the art on ASVspoof 2021 DF sits at 2–7 %. Our numbers describe the pipeline working — they are not a claim about ElevenLabs, RVC or XTTS, which we have not tested against. ml/train.py reads ASVspoof and In-the-Wild layouts unchanged; retraining is one command, and it is the first item on the roadmap.

A real bug this caught

The first training run scored 100 % accuracy, 0 % EER. The cause: bona fide audio carried a DC offset of 4.4e-3 (an all-positive glottal pulse train) against 4e-5 for synthetic (the STFT round-trip removes DC). The classifier was reading our generator, not the speech. Adding a shared capture chain dropped that feature's importance from 0.55 to 0.038.

So we made it a build gate

detect_leakage runs before every training run and fails CI if any single feature reaches AUC ≥ 0.90 alone. A perfect score from a corpus artifact is worse than a mediocre score from real signal: it is confidently wrong and invisible in the headline number.

Seven failure modes we publish

High-quality commercial clones over a clean line · heavy codecs on genuine callers · utterances under 3 s · non-Indian accents, children, atypical voices · adversarial audio (unmitigated) · replay of a genuine recording (out of scope) · a classical speaker embedding weaker than ECAPA. All in docs/MODEL_CARD.md.

Scalability

  • Stateless inference; only the session map is in memory
  • Scale out with --scale backend=N + sticky routing on session id
  • Capacity ceiling returns 503 rather than degrading silently
  • Redis-backed sessions on the roadmap removes stickiness entirely

Privacy, enforced not promised

  • Default features-only — no audio touches disk
  • TTL sweeper hard-deletes; no soft-delete flag
  • Phone numbers salted-hashed; transcript digits redacted
  • DELETE /v1/sessions/{id} — right to erasure
  • Edge path proven by a runnable script, not asserted

A detector that is honest about being 85 % right is deployable; one that claims 99 % and hides its failure modes gets switched off in its first month — so we built the abstention, the calibration, the leakage gate and the model card first, and the accuracy will follow from real data.
